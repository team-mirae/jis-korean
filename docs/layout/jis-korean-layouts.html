JIS Korean layouts

Korean 2-set (두벌식) for JIS keyboards, and in Mode B for US ANSI. Every standard 2-set key still types what it always did; the extra keys add double consonants, compound vowels, Korean punctuation and F6–F10 conversions.

Number row
Double consonants ㅃ ㅉ ㄸ ㄲ ㅆ and compound vowels ㅟ ㅝ ㅚ ㅢ ㅘ. Digits are on Shift, so numbers type with Shift held.
Shift+Z is F10
Half-width letters (반각), since F10 isn't reachable everywhere. ㅋ stays on Z.
Punctuation
− ! ?, ^ ( ), ¥ em dash and ^, ; ~ and -, / middle dot ·, and 「 」 on Shift+[ and Shift+].
F6 한글 · F7 한자
F6 commits, and restores the Hangul after F9 or F10. F7 converts the last word to hanja.
F8 두벌식
Escape to standard 2-set over the stock JIS keys. Shift+F8 returns.
F9 전각 · F10 반각
The current word as full- or half-width letters. Pressing again cycles as typed → UPPER → Capitalized.

Standard 2-set, fixed by the core principle Same as Mode A Function keys US ANSI, unchanged by design
Same as Mode A
The letter keys, 1–0 with digits on Shift, Shift+Z as F10, and F6–F10. [ ] and 「 」 sit on the US [ and ] keys.
Everything else is US ANSI
By design, every other key types what it always did, so each symbol stays where a US typist expects it. Predictions read the keys as US ANSI too: Shift+2 suggests @, not ".
Use it as-is, or fork it
Keep Mode B as it is to keep every symbol where you know it, or fork it to fit your typing or your keyboard. A mode is two key tables (docs/modes.md).
Function keys
On an HHKB, F6–F10 are Fn+6 to Fn+0. On a MacBook they're fn+F6 to fn+F10, or the plain keys with “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” on.
